## Images

All twenty-three website photographs are downloaded stock photographs from Pexels. Sources and the license page are recorded in photo-sources.json. WebP files are saved locally in public/images. No AI-generated photography is used. Nine former material photos were replaced with cleaner material displays, stacked goods, and organized tools. Four dedicated warehouse reference photographs show pallet storage, construction material racks, bulk timber, and material handling. The client approved using warehouse reference photographs pending real company images. These are prominently labeled as stock references, not company premises or verified inventory; no capacity, stock value, delivery guarantee, or facility ownership is invented. The vector logo is original code-native artwork, inspired by joined architectural profiles.

The seventeen material categories are proposed showcase directions within the documented business scope. Specific inventory, product brands, test results, delivery regions, and certifications are not established by the registration document. Visible third-party packaging in stock images does not establish an authorized dealership or stocked brand. Copy and reference labels avoid presenting stock photography as verified company projects or facilities. Eight categories appear on the home page, with two larger lead photographs and six smaller entries. The complete library uses three columns on desktop, two on tablet, and one on mobile. Image titles and inquiry copy are matched to the final photographs: metal sections are not mislabeled as rebar, and metal tubing is not mislabeled as plastic pipe.

## Design direction

Architectural material index: daylight architectural photography; white and pale gray paper; deep green navigation and inquiry bands; rust accent used for material reference marks; quiet sans-serif identity with serif editorial supporting headings; straight edges and visible material texture.

Distinctive elements: C/S/H/P category index; drawing-like material reference labels; a browser-local project brief that collects material categories for inquiry. This differs from BlueSky Play's bright toy studio identity and interactive shape lab.

References studied from the workspace: BMW's image-first product clarity, IBM's aligned information grid, and Apple's restrained material photography. Brand names, assets, and proprietary typefaces were not copied.
